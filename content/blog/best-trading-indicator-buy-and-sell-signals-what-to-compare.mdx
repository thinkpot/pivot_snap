Comparing buy and sell signal indicators is harder than comparing most software, because the marketing pages all say roughly the same things — "accurate," "easy to use," "works on any market." The actual differences that matter show up in the details most listings skip. Here's what to actually compare side by side.

## Signal Logic: Visible or Black Box?

The single biggest differentiator between indicators is whether you can see why a signal fired. Some tools mark the structural or momentum reason behind each signal — a swing point, a divergence, a zone reaction. Others just place an arrow with no visible logic at all. The second type is much harder to evaluate, trust, or learn from, regardless of how it performs historically.

## Repainting Behavior

This is the comparison point most buyers skip and regret skipping. A repainting indicator redraws its own historical signals after new data arrives, which means every backtest screenshot showing "perfect" entries near tops and bottoms may not reflect what the tool actually would have shown you in real time. Before comparing win-rate claims between two indicators, test both using TradingView's bar replay feature — step through price candle by candle and check whether signals hold once confirmed, or shift after the fact.

## Signal Frequency vs. Signal Quality

Some indicators fire constantly; others fire rarely but with more supporting context. Neither is inherently better — it depends on your trading style. A scalper may want more frequent signals on lower timeframes; a swing trader benefits more from fewer, higher-conviction signals on higher timeframes. Compare indicators against your actual intended use, not against an abstract "more signals is better" assumption.

## Platform and Market Compatibility

Check which platform an indicator actually runs on (TradingView's Pine Script and MetaTrader's MQL5 are not interchangeable — see our [MT5 vs TradingView comparison](/blog/buy-sell-signal-indicator-mt5-vs-tradingview-key-differences) for the technical differences) and which markets it's actually built and tested for. An indicator tuned on forex majors may behave differently on crypto or low-liquidity stocks.

## Pricing Structure

Compare whether you're paying once or subscribing indefinitely, and what that price actually includes — ongoing updates, support, or just the initial script. A cheaper subscription can cost more over a year or two than a higher one-time price; run the actual math rather than comparing sticker prices directly.

## Proof: Live Demo vs. Cherry-Picked Screenshots

A seller confident in their indicator's real-time behavior should be willing to show it running live, not just backtested examples chosen to look good. ## A Practical Comparison Checklist

- **Signal logic** — a weak indicator gives you an unexplained arrow; a stronger one shows a visible structural or momentum reason.
- **Repainting** — a weak indicator redraws history with an untested claim; a stronger one passes a bar-replay test.
- **Frequency** — a weak indicator fires constantly regardless of context; a stronger one matches your trading style and timeframe.
- **Platform fit** — a weak indicator is vague about compatibility; a stronger one is clear about which platform and markets it's built for.
- **Pricing** — a weak indicator is opaque or an ongoing cost without clear value; a stronger one is transparent, either one-time or a clearly justified subscription.
- **Proof** — a weak indicator only shows backtests; a stronger one has a live demo available.

## Common Mistakes When Comparing Indicators

- **Comparing marketing claims instead of testing behavior.** Every seller claims accuracy; the repainting test and a live demo tell you more than the sales copy.
- **Choosing based on price alone.** The cheapest option isn't automatically the best value if it lacks visible logic or repaints.
- **Ignoring platform fit.** An indicator built for one platform's calculation engine (Pine Script vs. MQL5, for example) won't produce identical results if ported casually to the other.
- **Not checking if the tool matches your actual timeframe and market.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a more expensive buy/sell indicator always better?**
No — price often reflects marketing and packaging as much as actual signal quality. The comparison points above (repainting, visible logic, platform fit) matter more than the price tag alone.

**How do I fairly compare two indicators' win rates?**
Be skeptical of any win-rate claim that isn't backed by a verifiable, non-repainting track record. Testing both indicators yourself with bar replay, over the same historical period, is a more reliable comparison than trusting marketing statistics.

**Should I compare indicators within the same platform, or across platforms?**
Comparing within the same platform (TradingView to TradingView, for example) is more apples-to-apples, since calculation engines and default settings can differ meaningfully between platforms like TradingView and MetaTrader.
